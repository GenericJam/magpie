# MIDI: in, out and clock in the engine, ports and devices in an in-repo plugin

- Date: 2026-11-13
- Status: accepted (built on the `midi` branch)

## Context

HANDOFF §1 and protocol §4: a virtual MIDI port "Magpie" (in and out) plus
USB and Bluetooth-LE MIDI devices; note-on plays the pad whose `midi_note`
matches (velocity passes through) on the kit's `in_channel`, keys pads play
chromatically around their root, note-off releases gate and keys pads;
pads send their `midi_note` when hit by touch or by the sequencer; clock
`internal`, `follow` (tempo, start/stop and song position from incoming
MIDI clock) or `send` (24 PPQN, start/stop, song position out). Humdinger
sends ordinary notes; nothing Magpie-specific.

Constraints from the engine (`decisions/2026-11-12-audio-engine-design.md`):
the Android BEAM runs one scheduler, so nothing that keeps time may go
through it. The sequencer lives in the AAudio callback, positions are
linear in engine frames, and the output timestamp maps engine frames onto
CLOCK_MONOTONIC. Android's MIDI API (`android.media.midi`) is Java only:
there is no NDK MIDI before API 29 (`AMidi`, and it can't host a
`MidiDeviceService` or open Bluetooth devices), and Magpie's minSdk is 28.

Prior art: Humdinger's `plugins/humdinger_midi_port` (a `MidiDeviceService`
port, the device watcher, Bluetooth-MIDI scan and connect, the
`app_process` probe) and `mob_midi` (open and send to devices from the
BEAM). mob_midi sends through the BEAM with the time of the call, has no
receive path with timestamps and no hot-plug on Android, so it can't stamp
from the audio clock or feed the audio thread.

## Decision

- **No MIDI message passes through the BEAM.** Kotlin receives (the port's
  input, every opened device's outputs) and hands the bytes with their
  timestamp (`System.nanoTime`, CLOCK_MONOTONIC) straight to C through a
  registered native; C parses them (running status, real-time bytes inside
  messages, SysEx skipped, one parser per source) and queues one command
  per message on the engine's command queue. Output goes the other way:
  the renderer stamps each message with the engine frame it belongs to, a
  C thread converts the frame to CLOCK_MONOTONIC through the output
  timestamp, sleeps until then and calls Kotlin, which sends to the port
  and every opened device with that timestamp. The BEAM only configures
  (the kit's mapping and clock mode), manages devices and hears about
  tempo and transport changes.
- **The renderer does the mapping, from a table the kit compiles to.**
  `Magpie.Midi.Map.config(kit)` (pure Elixir, host-tested) turns the kit
  into `in_channel`, the out channel, the clock mode, per note the pads it
  plays and per pad the note it sends; `Engine.set_midi/1` hands it to the
  renderer as one allocated table swapped in like a pattern. KitSession
  pushes it whenever the kit changes. The rule: a note plays every pad
  (any bank) whose `midi_note` it is, unless that pad is a keys pad; a note
  that no such pad claims goes to every keys pad, which plays it
  chromatically from its root. So a drum kit stays a drum kit and a keys
  pad takes the rest of the keyboard (Humdinger's melody), and a clash is
  solved by moving a pad's note in the PadSheet. Pads without a sample
  don't claim notes. Note-off (or note-on velocity 0) releases gate, loop
  and keys pads (keys: that note only); one-shots ring and toggles ignore
  it. A voice MIDI started is released by what came from the input,
  channel and note that started it, whatever the table says by then (a
  changed channel, a pad's note moved, a note given to another pad) and
  even with no stream running (a route change): its note-off, CC 123 (all
  notes off; CC 120, all sound off, fast) on its channel, or its input
  closing (unplugged, its port gone). The channel filter is `in_channel`
  (`all` or 1–16).
- **Live MIDI notes sound at once**, at the next buffer like a touch
  (≤ one burst), not at their timestamp plus a fixed delay: a player cares
  more about latency than about ≤ 5 ms of jitter. The timestamp still
  travels: while the sequencer plays, a MIDI note is a live-record hit
  stamped where it was played, and the in → heard latency is measured
  from it.
- **Notes out follow voices.** A voice started by touch or by the
  sequencer sends note-on (the pad's `midi_note`, keys pads the note they
  play, the velocity) on its first frame and note-off when it stops
  sounding at full: its release starts (finger lifted, step ended, choke,
  steal, toggle stopped, sequencer stopped) or a one-shot reaches its
  end. Every note-on has its note-off. Voices started by MIDI in send
  nothing (no echo, no feedback loop through the port). Out channel: the
  kit's `in_channel`, or 1 with `all`. Pads without a `midi_note` send
  nothing. One note is open per channel and note: a voice starting on a
  note another voice still holds sends that voice's note-off first (a
  receiver would otherwise end the new note on the old one's note-off).
  Note-offs are never dropped: the renderer's list and the out queue keep
  room for them, those of voices ended while no audio runs go out with the
  next block, and those left in the queue when a stream ends go out first
  when the next one starts (a note-on left there with its note-off was
  never heard: neither goes; one whose voice still holds the note goes,
  late, so its note-off has it). A note-on the full out queue refuses is
  taken back from its voice, so no note-off goes out for it either.
- **Clock send** is a tick every 4 sequencer ticks (96 PPQN → 24),
  computed from the sequencer's own position, so each tick lands on its
  exact frame through tempo changes, like the steps. Play sends song
  position 0 and Start on the play frame, then the first tick on that same
  frame; stop sends Stop. While stopped the clock keeps running at the
  tempo, so a follower shows it.
- **Clock follow** fits a line through the last 24 incoming ticks (their
  timestamps mapped to heard engine frames) and steers the sequencer onto
  it: on every tick the ticks-per-frame is set so the position meets the
  fitted line half a beat later (continuous, never backwards). The target
  is the master's tick *as received*, not a delayed copy: the steps are
  heard when the master's ticks arrive. Start (and Continue from a song
  position) arms the sequencer; the first tick after it starts it at that
  tick's heard frame, which is already a little in the past (the buffer
  the stream renders ahead), so the downbeat sounds that much late and
  everything after is on the grid. Stop stops. The followed tempo goes to
  the BEAM once a beat for display; the kit's tempo isn't changed.
  Ticks are numbered by time, not only counted, so ticks lost on the way
  (a cable, a Bluetooth dropout, a stalled sender) don't shift the grid.
  One gap can't tell lost ticks from a slower tempo, so a tick after a gap
  of about 2–3 ticks, or of more than 3.5, is held out of the fit (the
  sequencer goes on as it was) until the next gap decides: one tick later,
  ticks were lost and the held ticks are numbered so; a long gap repeated,
  the tempo dropped; 2–3 tick gaps go on being held (losses cluster) up to
  four in a row, then they are a slower tempo (from the second the
  sequencer goes at the gaps' pace, so a halved tempo doesn't leave it far
  ahead). Gaps are measured from where the fitted line puts the last tick,
  so one tick's jitter counts, not two. A master that pauses its clock
  without Stop is taken as having played on (the MIDI way to pause is
  Stop / Continue). Continue with no song position resumes on the 16th
  after the tick Stop came on, numbered as the clock running on while
  stopped settles the ticks held then (still held at Continue: taken as
  after lost ticks). A master that sends clock only while playing may
  restart at another tempo: Start or Continue after more than 3.5 ticks
  of silence starts a fresh line (the old one would read half the tempo's
  gaps as lost ticks, double's ticks as bursts) and a fresh count; the old
  tempo still judges gaps until the new line has 6 ticks, so a tick lost
  just after is renumbered (the very tick after Start lost isn't: the
  next is taken as the first). The clock and
  transport of one input are followed (the Magpie port, a USB or Bluetooth
  device): the first to send a tick or transport message, until another
  input starts its transport (Start; song position or Continue while
  stopped), which takes over from a fresh fit. Two masters' ticks merged
  would read as one at twice the tempo, and another device's Stop would
  stop the run; the followed input's going away frees it (stopped: its fit
  forgotten; playing: the sequencer runs on as through a dropout).
- **Ports and devices: an in-repo Kotlin-only plugin,
  `plugins/magpie_midi`**, as `magpie_capture` is: the manifest service
  and its `res/xml` device are only shippable through a plugin; the
  `magpie_audio` NIF registers its natives. Kotlin holds
  `MagpieMidiService` (device "Magpie", one input and one output port),
  the device watcher (hot-plug), opening and closing devices (all output
  ports in, input port 0 out), the Bluetooth-MIDI scan and connect
  (ported from Humdinger, `:midi_bluetooth` permission capability), and
  the send to every destination. Not mob_midi: see Context.
- **Which devices are open is `Magpie.Midi`'s policy** (a GenServer from
  `App.on_start`): USB and Bluetooth devices open when they appear, other
  apps' virtual ports (Humdinger's "Humdinger") only when the user turns
  them on, since opening one starts that app's service. A device the user
  turns on or off is remembered by name (`Mob.State`).
- **Settings surface**: a plain `Magpie.MidiScreen` (MIDI in channel,
  clock, devices, Bluetooth scan) behind the magpie menu button. Not
  designed; the Connect board only shows the two summary rows. Replace it
  when the design exists.

## Consequences

- A MIDI note reaches the renderer without a BEAM hop: receive thread →
  native → command queue → next callback. A busy BEAM (a sample loading,
  a screen rendering) never delays MIDI in or out.
- Clock out is exact to the frame in the engine; its wall-clock accuracy
  is the output timestamp's and the out thread's wake-up. Receivers that
  honour MIDI timestamps get the stamp itself.
- Following adds no latency after the downbeat but can't make the
  downbeat itself early: it is late by the stream's write-ahead.
- The mapping lives in two places: Elixir compiles the table (tested
  there), C applies it (tested on the host); the table is the contract.
- The out queue is the renderer's: if Kotlin's send blocks (a client of
  the port that stops reading), MIDI out stalls and the queue drops, but
  audio never waits. Ticks more than 20 ms late are dropped rather than
  sent in a burst; notes never are.
